# Each guest is a member of their own

Superseded by [ADR 0008](/docs/adr/0008-guests-share-one-guest-member-again.md), which keeps its guest budget and sign-in log.

Superseding [ADR 0005](/docs/adr/0005-guests-share-one-member-and-network.md). "Sign in as a guest" makes a new guest member for each sign-in, a temporary member with a generated name, who owns the shared guest network together with every other guest member, and can do everything any member can: rename themselves, sign out everywhere, delete their account, create networks, invite people, join a network by invite, manage API keys and a Qloo key, and delete a network they own. Adding a passkey keeps the account: it stops being a guest member and becomes an ordinary member, with a recovery code, and counts as a sign-up. A guest member with no passkey adds that first passkey, or deletes their account, without a step-up, since the session is the only proof they have. A guest member that hasn't kept the account is removed by an hourly cron once its one-day session has ended, with every network only expiring guests own and every API key and invite link it made.

Every daily cap is still one budget for all guests together, the guest budget, whoever the guest is and wherever they sign in from: the guest network and every network a guest member creates count under the guest network's key. A network a guest member creates gets an ID that starts with `guest-`, as the guest network's does, so its agents know which key to count under without a lookup. Its own Qloo gateway instance, if a guest adds a key, stays its own. Guest sign-ins keep their one exact limit a minute for everyone, and gain one a day that keeps the directory inside the database's free write quota, both counted from a log of guest sign-ins that deleting sessions or accounts can't shrink. A browser that already holds a live session isn't signed in as a guest again, so no button signs a member out. Invited sign-ups from invite links that guest members made share one daily count, as one maker's do.

We chose a member per guest over one shared member, which ADR 0005 chose, because a shared member can't do everything without one guest locking every other out or reaching past the guest network: deleting the account or the guest network, signing everyone out, adding a passkey or a recovery code that takes the account over, or joining a real network on behalf of every anonymous guest. ADR 0005 rejected a member per guest because each would multiply the caps a member gets; counting every guest member's networks under one key keeps the budget global, and removing expired guest members bounds the rows they add.

## Consequences

- Guest members can demote, remove, and lock out one another in the guest network, as owners of any network can. The guest network is exempt from the last-owner rule, since sign-in keeps adding owners. Deleting it removes it for every guest member signed in; the next guest sign-in makes it again, and the daily cron clears it.
- A network a guest member creates stays on the guest budget for good, even after its guest keeps their account, since a network's ID can't change.
- Real members can join a guest member's network by invite; it's removed with the guest member unless the guest adds a passkey first.
- The guest network's member list grows with every guest sign-in until the daily cron removes expired guest members.
